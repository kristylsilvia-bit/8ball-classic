Topic 01
Neolithic Age & Ancient Civilizations
⌄
I. The Neolithic Age (8000–3000 BC)

Also called the New Stone Age — one of the most important turning points in human history.

Key Developments
  • Humans discovered agriculture (farming) and learned to domesticate animals for food.
  • Humans switched from being nomads (wanderers who hunted and gathered) to settled farmers living in permanent villages.
  • Farming created a steady food surplus — more food than needed to survive.
  • Permanent villages eventually grew into civilizations.
  • This shift was so massive it's called the Neolithic Revolution.
II. What is a Civilization?

A civilization is a complex, highly organized society. It includes all six of these features:

Government Social Classes Job Specialization Food Surplus Writing System Religious Beliefs

The first civilizations emerged around 3000 BC in river valleys. Why river valleys?

  • Flooding rivers deposited silt — rich soil perfect for farming.
  • People could irrigate (water) crops from the river.
  • Rivers provided a source of transportation.
III. The First River Valley Civilizations (c. 3000–1000 BC)
Egypt
Location: Northeastern Africa
River: Nile River

Achievements:
  • Hieroglyphics — picture-symbol writing
  • Pyramids — massive structures to bury Pharaohs (kings)
Mesopotamia (Sumer)
Location: Middle East
Rivers: Tigris & Euphrates

Achievements:
  • Cuneiform — wedge-shaped symbol writing
  • Code of Hammurabi — oldest known written laws; known for harsh punishments: "an eye for an eye"
Harappan Civilization
Location: India
River: Indus River

Achievement: Organized, well-planned urban (city) areas

Note: Early Indian societies were strongly affected by seasonal monsoons — winds bringing rain needed for farming (but too much caused flooding).
China
Rivers: Yellow (Huang He) & Yangtze

Early Chinese societies were very isolated from other civilizations — surrounded by natural barriers (mountains and deserts).
Common Features of Ancient Societies
  • All existed in river valley regions.
  • All were polytheistic — believed in many gods associated with nature (Sun God, Rain God, etc.).
  • All had traditional economies:
    • Barter — trade without money
    • Subsistence agriculture — farming only to feed one's own family, not for profit
    • Children typically had the same job as their parents (usually farming)
Topic 02
Classical Civilizations
⌄
I. Introduction (c. 1000 BC – 500 AD)

Classical civilizations were more advanced and more recent than ancient ones. Most had a Golden Age — a period of great achievements in art, literature, math, and science.

II. Greece
  • Located on a peninsula in southeastern Europe with very mountainous geography.
  • Because of mountains, Greece was divided into many independent city-states (each called a polis), each with its own government and land.
Sparta
  • Military society — men spent almost all their lives training for warfare.
  • Very little freedom for the people.
Athens
  • Home of the world's first democracy — a government where people can vote.
  • Direct democracy: all male citizens born in Athens voted directly on laws.
  • Known for great philosophers: Socrates, Aristotle, Plato
  • Writers like Homer
Religion & Culture
  • Polytheistic — believed in many nature gods.
  • Olympic Games — held every 4 years to honor their god Zeus.
Alexander the Great
  • Conquered Greece, Egypt, Persia (Iran), and part of India.
  • Spread Hellenistic culture — a mixture of Greek, Egyptian, Persian, and Indian culture throughout conquered lands.
III. Rome
  • Located on the peninsula of Italy.
  • Began as a small city-state; grew into an empire by conquering regions surrounding the Mediterranean Sea (Western Europe, North Africa, Greece, Anatolia, and Western Asia).
Key Features & Achievements
  • Roads & Trade Networks — united the empire; the Mediterranean Sea connected all territories.
  • Twelve Tables of Rome — written laws displayed publicly; created stability (though they favored the wealthy).
  • Legal Ideas — concepts like "innocent until proven guilty" that we still use today.
  • Pax Romana ("Roman Peace") — 200-year Golden Age featuring trade and great achievements in art, literature, math, and science.
IV. India — Classical Period

Ruled by two dynasties: the Maurya Dynasty and the Gupta Dynasty.

Maurya Dynasty
Asoka: Famous ruler who wrote laws on rock pillars displayed throughout India. Converted to Buddhism; known for his kind treatment of people and animals.
Gupta Dynasty — India's Golden Age
  • Invention of zero in mathematics
  • Development of Sanskrit writing
  • Beautiful Buddhist paintings and art
Caste System

The caste system was India's rigid social hierarchy — people were born into a social class and stayed there for life.

LevelCasteRole
1 (Highest)BrahminsPriests
2KshatriyasWarriors
3VaisyasMerchants & Artisans
4SudrasLaborers
LowestUntouchablesWorst, most undesirable jobs

The caste system provided order and structure to society. It is closely associated with Hinduism.

V. China — Classical Period
Qin Dynasty (only 15 years)
Based government on Legalism — humans are evil; harsh punishments are needed to keep order. Similar idea to the Code of Hammurabi.
Han Dynasty (400 years)
  • Roads — grew wealthy through trade (similar to Rome).
  • Civil Service System — government positions only given to those who passed difficult exams. China was the first civilization to use this system.
VI. The Silk Road
  • A 4,000-mile trade route stretching from China in the east to the Mediterranean Sea in the west.
  • Connected the different classical civilizations (and later ones too).
Cultural Diffusion Cultural diffusion is the exchange of goods and ideas between societies. The Silk Road was the ultimate example — spreading products (like silk) and religious ideas (like Buddhism) between civilizations.
Topic 03
World Religions & Philosophies
⌄
I. The Three Monotheistic Religions

Monotheism = belief in only one God. The three main monotheistic religions are Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.

Codes of Conduct (Behavior)
  • Ten Commandments — for Jews and Christians (Do not kill, do not steal, do not worship false gods, etc.)
  • Five Pillars of Islam — for Muslims (pilgrimage to Mecca, pray 5 times daily, fasting, charity, declaration of faith)
II. Religions of India
Hinduism
  • Reincarnation — after death, the soul is reborn into another body; humans cycle through many lives.
  • Karma — all good and bad deeds during a lifetime. Good karma = reborn at a higher level. Bad karma = reborn lower.
  • Goal: achieve Moksha — ending the cycle of death and rebirth.
  • Closely tied to the Caste System — the class you're born into reflects karma from a past life.
Buddhism
  • Founded by Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha).
  • Similar to Hinduism — believes in Reincarnation and Karma.
  • Goal: achieve Nirvana — ending the cycle of death and rebirth.
  • Four Noble Truths: all life is suffering; suffering is caused by selfish desires.
  • Eightfold Path: a code of behavior — resist evil, act kindly, meditate, etc.
Quick Comparison: Hinduism vs. Buddhism Both believe in Reincarnation and Karma. Hinduism's goal = Moksha. Buddhism's goal = Nirvana. Both are very similar concepts — ending the cycle of rebirth.
III. Philosophies of China
Legalism
Humans are naturally evil; harsh punishments are necessary to prevent crime and keep order in society. (Compare to the Code of Hammurabi in Mesopotamia — same harsh idea.)
Confucianism
Major Chinese philosophy. Key teachings:
  • Five Relationships: everyone has specific roles and obligations to maintain order — subjects obey rulers, wives obey husbands, children obey parents.
  • Filial Piety: people must honor and respect their elders.
IV. Nature Religions

Nature religions believe that both living and non-living things (trees, mountains, rivers, rocks, animals) have a spirit.

Shinto
Practiced in Japan. Believes in nature spirits.
Animism
Practiced in Africa. Believes in nature spirits.
Regents Note Both Shinto and Animism believe in nature spirits — this is a very common Regents exam question!
Topic 04
Legal Systems of the Ancient & Classical Worlds
⌄
Legal System Where Used Key Feature
Code of Hammurabi Ancient Mesopotamia Harsh punishments — "an eye for an eye"
Twelve Tables of Rome Roman Empire Written laws displayed publicly; favored the wealthy; created stability
Justinian Code Byzantine Empire Based on old Roman law; later adopted by many European civilizations
What all 3 legal systems have in common — Regents Favorite!
  • They are all standardized (written) sets of laws.
  • They each concern the relationship between the state (government) and the individual.
  • They all helped create stability — an orderly society — because people could learn what the laws were.
Topic 05
The Byzantine Empire
⌄
Byzantine Empire (c. 500–1453 AD)

In 476 AD, the western Roman Empire was conquered by Germanic tribes. The eastern portion survived and became the Byzantine Empire.

Key Features & Achievements
  • People were mainly Greek in language and culture.
  • Constantinople — the capital city; located along major waterways; a crossroads of Europe and Asia, making it ideal for trade.
  • Eastern Orthodox Christianity — the branch of Christianity practiced there. (Famous church: the Hagia Sophia)
  • Justinian Code — written laws created by Emperor Justinian; based on Roman law; later adopted by European civilizations.
  • Preserved Greek & Roman Culture — Byzantines saved and passed on important texts from Greece and Rome.
Cultural Diffusion — Impact on Russia
Byzantine Empire → Russia Through contact with Byzantium, Russia received two major things still present today:
  • The Eastern Orthodox Religion
  • The Cyrillic Alphabet (writing system used in Russia today)
Topic 06
The Golden Age of Islam
⌄
I. Islam — Key Facts
  • Monotheistic religion — one God called Allah.
  • Followers are called Muslims.
  • Holy Book: the Koran (Qu'ran).
  • Five Pillars — religious and moral duties of all Muslims (pilgrimage to Mecca, pray 5 times daily, etc.).
  • Muhammad (570–632 AD) — founder of Islam; born in Mecca (holiest city); believed by Muslims to be the last of God's prophets (messengers).
II. The Golden Age of Islam (c. 700–1200 AD)

After Muhammad's death, Muslim armies swept out of the Arabian peninsula and conquered vast areas — much of the Middle East, North Africa, and even southern Spain. During this period, amazing achievements were made:

Medicine
Creation of medical encyclopedias
Mathematics
Improvements in math, especially algebra
Art
Calligraphy (beautiful writing), woolen carpets, textiles
Topic 07
The Middle Ages
⌄
I. Introduction (400–1400 AD)

The Middle Ages is the period of European history after the collapse of the Roman Empire and before the Renaissance.

II. Feudalism

Feudalism was the political system of the Middle Ages — kings gave land to nobles in return for their loyalty and military service.

  • Nobles had to serve as knights (warriors on horseback) and follow chivalry (a code of loyalty and bravery).
  • Feudalism was decentralized — kings shared/gave away power to nobles who each controlled their own local areas.
  • Land was the basis of wealth and power — more land = more power.
  • Feudalism brought social stability, order, and structure to the Middle Ages.
III. Manorialism
  • Most people lived on manors — large areas of land owned by a noble.
  • Each manor contained homes, farmland, artisans, water, and serfs — peasants who couldn't leave the land and did farm labor for the noble.
  • Manors were mostly self-sufficient (met their own needs), so trade decreased during the Middle Ages.
IV. Religion in the Middle Ages

The Roman Catholic Church (especially the Pope) was extremely wealthy and powerful in Western Europe during this period.

V. The Crusades (1100–1300)

Religious wars in which Christians from Europe fought to reclaim the Holy Land (a sacred area of the Middle East, including Jerusalem) from Muslims.

Causes
  • Europeans believed they'd be forgiven for sins if they fought for God.
  • Europeans believed the Holy Land shouldn't be controlled by Muslims.
  • Many poor Europeans wanted to escape feudalism.
  • Many hoped to gain wealth from the Middle East.
Effects
  • Trade between Europe and the Middle East increased (Italian cities like Venice gained control of much of this trade).
  • Europeans learned about Muslim achievements in math, science, medicine, and art.
  • Feudalism began to decline — many nobles were killed; many serfs escaped.
Topic 08
Golden Age of China — Tang & Song Dynasties
⌄
Tang & Song Dynasties (600s–1200s)

China's Golden Age — a period of massive achievements in the arts, science, math, and literature.

Key Inventions & Achievements
Gunpowder
Invented in China; later adopted by civilizations in Europe and the Middle East.
Compass
Improved sailing and navigation by sea — made long voyages possible.
Block Printing
Ink placed on carved wooden blocks pressed onto paper — the forerunner of the printing press.
Porcelain Art
Beautiful works of art created using fine porcelain clay.
Other Key Facts
  • Long-distance trade on land (Silk Road) and by sea (from port city of Canton).
  • Cultural Diffusion: Buddhism became popular in China during this period — spread via the Silk Road.
  • Continued to use civil service exams to select highly qualified government workers.
Topic 09
African Civilizations
⌄
I. Introduction to Africa
  • Very diverse geography: deserts (Sahara), savanna (grasslands), and rainforests.
  • Due to its varied geography, Africa has many different cultures — no two societies are exactly alike.
  • Desertification — the Sahara continues to grow, reducing available farmland. A current problem for Africa.
  • Traditional religion: Animism — believes all living and non-living things in nature have a spirit. (Similar to Shinto in Japan.)
II. Bantu Migrations (500 BC – 1500 AD)

One of the largest migrations in history. Due to a shortage of land, the Bantu people scattered throughout southern Africa over 2,000 years.

3 Things the Bantu Spread:
  • The Bantu language
  • Iron technology
  • Agricultural (farming) techniques
III. West African Civilizations (300–1400s)

Three of Africa's greatest civilizations all developed in West Africa:

Ghana Mali Songhai
Why did all three grow wealthy? All were located along the Trans-Saharan trade routes and participated in the Gold-Salt trade — trading their gold for the salt they needed to survive.
Mansa Musa — Most Famous Ruler of Mali
  • A devoted Muslim who helped spread Islam throughout West Africa.
  • Made a grand pilgrimage to Mecca in Saudi Arabia.
  • Turned the city of Timbuktu into a center of trade and Islamic learning.
Topic 10
The Mongols
⌄
I. Introduction (1100s–1400s)
  • The Mongols originated on the steppes (grassy plains) of Central Asia.
  • Lived as nomadic pastoralists — raised animals and migrated frequently in search of grazing lands.
II. The Mongol Empire
Largest Empire in History Under the brilliant military leadership of Genghis Khan, the Mongols built the LARGEST EMPIRE in all of history — stretching from China and Central Asia to Russia and much of the Middle East.
III. Marco Polo
  • Italian merchant who visited Mongol-ruled China and stayed for nearly 20 years.
  • Wrote extensively about his experiences in China.
  • Why his writings mattered:
    • Valuable primary sources about China during this period.
    • Increased European desire to trade with China for valuable goods.
IV. Why the Mongols Were Important
  • Trade and travel between Europe and Asia increased tremendously — Mongols kept peace and prevented violence along trade routes like the Silk Road.
  • Influence on Russia:
    • After conquering Russia, the Mongols kept it isolated from developments in Western Europe.
    • Taught the Russians how to have a centralized government — a strong government with one ruler in firm control.
  • First foreign group to completely conquer China.
  • Developed a tribute system — conquered areas were required to pay the Mongols money each year.
Topic 11
Transformation of Western Europe
⌄
I. The Black Death (1347–1351)

The Black Death (bubonic plague) killed 25 million people in Europe — nearly 1/3 of the population.

  • Started in East Asia (possibly China); spread to Europe via trade routes like the Silk Road.
Major Effects
  • Depopulation: Europe's population fell from 85 million to 60 million.
  • Feudalism continued to decline — serfs fled manors seeking better opportunities.
  • Trade temporarily decreased.
II. The Renaissance (1400–1600)

The Renaissance was the Golden Age of Western Europe.

  • Amazing achievements in arts, literature, and science.
  • Scholars studied ancient Greek and Roman art and books.
  • Humanism — a focus on humans and life on earth rather than on God and Heaven.
Why did the Renaissance start in Italy? Italian city-states (Venice, Florence) controlled Mediterranean trade and grew very wealthy. That wealth was used to support artists and scholars.
Famous Renaissance Artists
Donatello Leonardo da Vinci Michelangelo Raphael
Niccolo Machiavelli — "The Prince"
  • Rulers must have absolute power and do whatever is necessary to stay in power — even dishonest or cruel behavior.
  • "The end justifies the means" — leaders must do whatever it takes to help their nation.
III. The Protestant Reformation (1500s)

A religious revolution that challenged the Catholic Church and led to further division of Christianity.

Causes
  • Europeans angry that the Church was too focused on money and power.
  • Europeans angry about indulgences — reductions in punishment sold by the Church.
Key Leaders
  • Martin Luther — wrote the 95 Theses: 95 arguments against indulgences.
  • Henry VIII — split from the Church because he wanted a divorce and the Pope refused.
  • John Calvin — believed in predestination (God already knows who will be saved).
Effects
  • Power of the Catholic Church and the Pope decreased.
  • Power of monarchs increased.
  • Religious unity in Europe ended — now both Catholics and Protestants.
IV. The Printing Press

Invented by Johannes Gutenberg. Its importance:

  • Helped spread new ideas quickly across Europe.
  • Helped spread the Protestant Reformation — people like Martin Luther could print and distribute their writings.
  • Increased literacy — books became easier and cheaper to obtain.
V. The Scientific Revolution

A dramatic change in how people understood the world — science and reason (logic) were used to explain nature, not just the Bible or the Church.

PersonContribution
CopernicusHeliocentric Theory — planets revolve around the sun (not Earth)
GalileoProved Copernicus correct; put on trial by the Catholic Church for contradicting its teachings
Isaac NewtonDeveloped calculus and the theory of gravity
KeplerHelped discover how planets move
DescartesMathematician, scientist, and philosopher
Key Effects of the Scientific Revolution
  • Spread of new ideas throughout Europe.
  • Challenged the Catholic Church — scientists proved many Church teachings were incorrect.
  • Directly led to the Enlightenment.
Topic 12
Pre-Columbian Civilizations of the Americas
⌄
I. Introduction (c. 300–1500s AD)

Pre-Columbian civilizations are the societies that developed in the Americas before the arrival of Europeans. The three main ones — Maya, Aztec, and Inca — were all very organized and advanced.

II. The Three Civilizations
The Maya
Location: Lowland Mesoamerica (Mexico & Central America)

Achievements:
  • Developed a calendar and form of writing
  • Invented the use of zero in mathematics (like India's Gupta Dynasty)
The Aztec
Location: Mesoamerica (Mexico)
Capital: Tenochtitlan

Achievements:
  • Calendar and writing
  • Chinampas — "floating gardens" built in lakes to farm when land was scarce
Conquered by Hernan Cortez — 1521
The Inca
Location: Andes Mountains, Peru (South America)
Famous Site: Machu Picchu

Achievements:
  • Vast network of roads and footbridges (like the Romans)
  • Terrace Farming — flat areas carved into mountain slopes for farming
Conquered by Francisco Pizarro — 1532
Regents Note — Agricultural Adaptation Both the Aztecs (chinampas) and the Inca (terrace farming) used creative agricultural techniques to adapt to their geographic environments. Japan also used terrace farming. This is a common comparison question!
Topic 13
Age of Exploration & Encounter
⌄
I. Introduction (1400s–1600s)

The period when Europeans began sea voyages, reached the Americas, and began to colonize (take over) lands in the Americas, Africa, and Asia. Spain and Portugal led the way.

Causes of Exploration
  • Europeans wanted greater access to spices and Asian products.
  • Needed sea routes to Asia — land routes were controlled by Muslims.
  • Improvements in navigation technology: compass, astrolabe, cartography (map-making), and the lateen sail.
II. Key Explorers
ExplorerAchievement
Bartholomeu DiasFirst to round the Cape of Good Hope (southern tip of Africa)
Vasco da GamaFirst to reach India by sailing around the Cape of Good Hope
Christopher ColumbusFirst European explorer to reach the Americas
Ferdinand MagellanFirst to circumnavigate (sail around) the entire globe
III. Effects of Exploration
The Americas
  • Spanish and Portuguese colonized land — controlled areas called colonies.
  • Encomienda System — Native Americans forced to work on Spanish plantations and in mines; Spanish landowners had total control over them.
  • Millions of Native Americans died from diseases (like smallpox) brought by Europeans.
Africa
  • Europeans took enslaved Africans to the Americas to work on plantations.
  • Middle Passage — the voyage of enslaved Africans from Africa to the Americas.
Mercantilism
  • Mercantilism — the idea that colonies exist only to make the Mother Country wealthy.
  • Europeans took raw materials (cotton) from colonies, then sold finished products (clothing) back to the colonies.
Triangular Trade

The Atlantic trade route: Europeans went to Africa to get enslaved people → brought enslaved people to the Americas → returned to Europe with American goods.

Columbian Exchange
Columbian Exchange The exchange of people, plants, animals, ideas, and technology between the "Old World" (Europe) and the "New World" (Americas). The ultimate example of cultural diffusion.
Europe → AmericasAmericas → Europe
Wheat, sugar, bananas, horses, chickens,

IV. Commercial Revolution (1500s–1600s)

New forms of business introduced during exploration:

  • Joint-stock companies — investors combined money to fund trading projects (shared profit and risk).
  • Expansion of banking.
  • Capitalism — profits from one project reinvested in others to make more money.
Topic 14
The Travelers of Global I
⌄
Marco Polo
Italian merchant who traveled to China (via the Silk Road) when it was ruled by the Mongols. Stayed for nearly 20 years. Wrote extensively about his experiences.
Ibn Battuta
Arab/Muslim explorer who traveled a total of 75,000 miles, visiting lands in Africa, Asia, and Europe. One of the greatest travelers in history.
Zheng He
Chinese explorer of the Ming Dynasty who sailed the Indian and Pacific Oceans. Visited Southeast Asia, India, and the western coast of Africa.
Why all 3 Travelers were Important
  • They wrote extensively — their writings are valuable primary sources about the lands they visited.
  • The contacts they made helped stimulate (increase) trade between different regions of the world.
Topic 15
The Age of Absolutism
⌄
I. Overview (1600s–1700s)

The Age of Absolutism was the period when European nations were governed by absolute monarchs — kings who had total control over the nations they ruled.

Key Characteristics of Absolute Monarchs
  • Centralized political control — did not share power with anyone else.
  • Believed in Divine Right — kings received their power to rule directly from God.
  • Believed their power was unlimited — made laws without the consent (approval) of the people.
II. Important Absolute Monarchs
MonarchCountryNotable For
Peter the Great Russia Westernized Russia — imitated Western European customs to strengthen Russia; also expanded its territory
Louis XIV France Held absolute power; expanded France's territory
Philip II Spain Absolute monarch of Spain
Henry VIII England Separated from the Catholic Church; founded the Church of England
Suleiman the Magnificent Ottoman Empire Expanded the Ottoman Empire's territory significantly
Regents Note Peter the Great, Louis XIV, and Suleiman the Magnificent all expanded the territory of their nations by conquering neighboring lands. This is a common Regents comparison.
